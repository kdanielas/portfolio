Case study

Reimagining Digital Investing with AI-Powered Guidance

BMO InvestorLine gives investors full control — no advisor required. But control without guidance creates friction at the moments that matter most. I joined a 5-month engagement to redesign how self-directed investors understand their portfolio and act with confidence.

I led design on Journey 2 — How's My Portfolio Doing — and collaborated across onboarding and portfolio management to keep all three journeys aligned. My work covered discovery synthesis, user flows, wireframes, and high-fidelity prototypes explored through user testing.

My Role

  • Lead Designer — Journey 2 (Portfolio Understanding)
  • Contributed across all 3 journeys for design alignment
  • Collaborated with PM, engineers, and cross-functional stakeholders

Team

Lead UX Strategist + me (Product Designer)

01 — The Challenge

How might we help users make better financial decisions without taking control away from them?

Two weeks of discovery workshops surfaced the same friction, over and over:

Portfolio data was fragmented — users couldn't see the full picture
Trades lacked clarity on risk, diversification, and tax impact
Educational content existed but was disconnected from real decision moments
Users couldn't tell which recommendations were actually personalized to them
The friction eroded confidence in trading and portfolio management overall
02 — Framing the Opportunity

Users don’t want automation. They want confidence.

The goal wasn't to build an AI. It was to make the platform feel like it was on the user's side. We reframed the experience around three things:

Surfacing the right insight at the right moment
Making consequences visible before users commit
Guiding without dictating
03 — Approach

Stakeholder alignment was the hardest part. The project extended from January to March — prototypes and storytelling artifacts were our main tool for building a shared direction.

Key Activities Included:

2-week discovery sprint with stakeholders across product, engineering, design, and business
Journey mapping across all 3 journeys to find the highest-friction decision moments
Rapid concept exploration — ideas on paper before committing to pixels
Usability testing to validate direction before going high-fidelity
3 experience principles defined to keep design decisions consistent across all journeys

The constant question: does this help users understand what's happening and feel confident taking action?

04 — Users

BMO defined two audiences. I mapped their shared needs and the tensions between them — they drove every design decision.

👴🏼

Experienced wealth investors

  • Baby boomers managing significant household assets
  • Want full portfolio visibility and fast access to insights
  • Trust their judgment — don't want to feel patronized
👦🏻

Next-gen digital investors

  • Digital natives inheriting or building their own wealth
  • Expect intuitive, self-service experiences
  • New to investing — need guidance to act without calling an advisor
Shared need: Understand what's happening in their portfolio, know what to do next, and feel confident enough to act — without calling an advisor.
05 — The Solution

From transaction-first investing to guided, confidence- building decision- making

Not a chatbot. A guidance layer woven into the moments that matter — embedded in workflows users already know.

Every decision came back to one question: does this help users understand what's happening and act with confidence?

The Solution

01 Embedded AI 02 Intelligence 03 Simulation 04 Exploration
01

Embedded AI Assistant

Goal: Always available, never intrusive.

Explains decisions in plain language
Answers questions in context — not in a separate screen
Surfaces next steps based on the user's portfolio
Builds trust through transparency — not just answers, but reasoning

Design decision

In early concepts we explored a dedicated AI hub — a separate screen for guidance. Users ignored it. Moving guidance inline, to the exact moment a decision is being made, is what made it feel useful.

02

Pre-Trade Intelligence

Goal: Help users act with confidence

Executing a trade is the highest-stakes moment in the experience. We designed a layer that surfaces what matters before users commit:

Trade impact on risk and diversification
Scenario insights — what changes if I proceed?
Contextual explanations tied to the user's portfolio
"How will this affect my portfolio balance?"
"What should I consider before making this trade?"

Design decision

Most platforms stop at the transaction mechanic. We designed one step earlier — surfacing impact on risk, diversification, and goals before the user clicks confirm.

03

Learn by Doing

Safe Simulation

Try a trade, see the impact, build confidence — then decide. A risk-free space to practice before putting real money on the line.

Why it matters:

Reduces fear of making the wrong move
Builds confidence through experimentation, not instruction

Design decision

New investors didn't fear the platform — they feared making the wrong move. A risk-free sandbox lowered that barrier without removing their sense of control.

04

Scenario Exploration

What If

Goal: Help users explore "What If" outcomes before committing.

Design decision:

A "What If?" tool that makes the consequences of decisions visible before users commit.
Impact: Users shift from reactive to proactive thinking

Design decision

Preserving autonomy was a core principle. AI doesn't decide — it shows users the impact of their choices so they can decide for themselves.

06 — Roadmap & MVP Definition

To bring the vision to life, we defined a phased approach focused on delivering value at key moments in the investing journey — rather than launching a fully comprehensive AI system all at once.

PHASE — 1

Understanding Your Portfolio

Focus: Build confidence in core user journeys.

Key Features:

Portfolio health overview (asset allocation, diversification, risk, ratings).
Plain-language explanations of portfolio performance.
Contextual prompts introducing AI-assisted guidance.

Outcome: Users gain a clear understanding of their portfolio and what to do next—without feeling overwhelmed.

PHASE — 2

Making Decisions with Guidance

Focus: Support high-stakes moments.

Key Features:

Pre-trade insights (impact on risk, diversification, alignment to goals).
Scenario exploration ("what happens if I make this change?").
AI-assisted explanations tied to user intent and portfolio context.
Guided flows for making portfolio adjustments.

Outcome: Users move from uncertainty to informed action with greater confidence.

PHASE — 3

Anticipation & Personalization

Focus: Anticipate user needs over time.

Key Features:

Intelligent alerts tied to portfolio health and market events.
Personalized recommendations based on goals and behavior.
Ongoing learning and education embedded in the experience.
A more adaptive AI assistant that evolves with the user.

Outcome: The platform shifts from reactive toolset to proactive financial partner.

Rather than treating AI as a standalone feature, this roadmap positions it as a progressively deepening layer of guidance — starting with explanation, expanding into decision support, and ultimately enabling personalized, proactive advice.

07 — Outcomes

Five months of work, ready for testing

  • High-fidelity concepts for all 3 journeys, explored through user testing
  • Cross-functional team aligned across product, engineering, and business
  • Experience concepts tied to KPIs: NPS, CSAT, trade volume, and advice adoption
  • Reusable guided decision-making patterns that scale across the platform
  • A phased roadmap — from near-term clarity to longer-term personalization
  • Handoff documentation for future development teams
08 — Key Takeaways
01

Stakeholder alignment is a design problem too

Prototypes weren’t just deliverables — they were the main tool for getting everyone on the same page.

02

Embed AI, don't centralize it

A dedicated AI screen gets ignored. Context matters more than capability.

03

Confidence is a design outcome

In financial UX, a user who understands what's happening is more valuable than one who has every data point.

09 — What I'd Improve Next

Always room to grow

east Run longitudinal studies to measure real impact on confidence and trade behavior
east Add user-controlled guidance levels — research showed people want different amounts of help
east Give the onboarding journey another round of concept testing — it needed more iteration time
Closing Thought

Designing for financial decision-making isn’t just about usability—it’s about responsibility.

The goal isn’t to make decisions for users, but to help them make better ones.
